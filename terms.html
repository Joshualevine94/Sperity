Last updated: 2 October 2026

These are the terms for using Sperity. They are written to be read, so they are short and in plain words. Using the app means agreeing to them.

In one sentence: Sperity shows you your own numbers and helps you think about them. It is not your bank, not your accountant, and not an adviser, and the figures in it are estimates based on what you and your bank tell it.

2. Who can use it

You must be 18 or over. The app is not designed for children and no data is knowingly collected from them.

You need your own account. Do not share your sign-in. If you share a household with someone, each of you keeps a separate sign-in.

3. What this is not

Not financial advice. Nothing in the app is a recommendation about what to do with your money. Projections, payoff plans, budgets and written summaries are arithmetic and observations, not advice. If you need advice, speak to someone qualified to give it.

Not a bank. Sperity holds no money and cannot move any. It cannot make a payment, transfer funds, or stop a payment going out.

Not a source of truth about your accounts. Your bank is. Where the app and your bank disagree, your bank is right.

4. The figures are estimates

Everything the app projects — what you can spend, when a pot will be full, when a debt clears, whether you run short before payday — is calculated from what you have entered and what your bank has reported. It is as good as that information and no better.

Specifically:

  • Bank data arrives through Plaid and may be delayed, incomplete, or wrong. Pending transactions change. Some accounts do not report at all.
  • A projection assumes bills recur as entered. A changed amount, a missed entry or an unexpected charge changes the answer.
  • AI features make mistakes. A merchant can be put in the wrong category and a PDF can be read incorrectly. Everything suggested is shown to you before it is saved, and it is worth checking.

Do not rely on the app alone for a decision that matters. Check your bank before anything with a consequence — a large purchase, a payment you cannot miss, a transfer you cannot reverse.
